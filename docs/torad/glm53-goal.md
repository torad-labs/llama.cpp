# GLM-5.3 Flash serving goal

Read this before starting any GLM-5.3 work, and check the work against it when it is done.

## The goal

GLM-5.3 Flash, the BoldingBuilds IQ3_XXS pack, served by this fork on four RTX PRO 6000 cards for 23 concurrent agent sessions of up to 256K tokens each (decided Nov 9; 36-40 sessions was the earlier target):

- **70 decode tokens/s per session, with all of them decoding at once** (50 is the floor worth serving).
- Context: one shared pool on the 4-bit latent cache (`--cache-type-k q4_0 --cache-type-v q4_0`, accepted Nov 9 with its Oct 30 measurement: same speed, KLD 1.26x the reorder floor at 196K-262K), 8,352 bytes a token mirrored on every card. The pool that holds 23 sessions of 256K (6,029,312 tokens) does not load yet: see cause 3.

Per-session decode rate at N concurrent sessions is the number. Aggregate is N times it. A single-session rate, a kernel time or a trace share is not progress toward the goal until it moves that number.

## Where a step goes

This section is the Nov 9 step of about 150 ms. The Nov 10 step of 70 ms is taken apart under The next number.

A step at 23 sessions is about 150 ms on four cards, on two cards and on `dp-4`. The layout does not move it, and neither do card-to-card reductions or weight reads:

- The cards are busy 23-30 % of the step: about 40 ms of work, which is the model's 26-37 ms. One host thread, the server's loop, is at 100 % of a core for the whole step, and every other thread is idle.
- The step grows by about 5.5 ms for each session added, in a straight line from 2 to 23.
- `LLAMA_TIME_DECODE=1` at 23 sessions: about four `llama_decode` calls a step (the target's pass and the MTP head's), about 25 ms each: `graph_compute` 69 %, `alloc_graph` 15.5 % (12-13 ms each time it runs, in under half the calls), 13 % in none of its phases, `build_graph` and `set_inputs` 1 % each. That is about 100 ms of the step inside `llama_decode` and about 60 ms outside it, in the server's own work for each slot.
- The sessions share one pool, the pool's padded length sets every graph's shapes, and 23 sessions cross a 256-cell padding every 5 steps where one session crosses it every ~115. Each crossing builds the step's four graphs again (the target's, the MTP head's two proposals and its catch-up), plans their memory, runs them uncaptured for that step and captures them the next. That is the `alloc_graph` share above (a graph planned in 34 of 64 decodes) and a session's target graph reused in 41 of its ~61 steps. Found Nov 9 on a fixture of the pack's block layout and vocabulary (46 blocks, 154,880 tokens, 23 sessions, two ranks on one local card, `GGML_GRAPH_TRACE=1`): 22 of 90 steps built a graph, and a crossing's two steps took 76 ms more than a 31 ms step.
- With every host cost gone the step would be the cards' 40 ms: 54 tok/s a session at 2.16 tokens a step. 70 needs that and either 3 tokens a step at the same rows or less work on the cards.
- The routed experts are about 21-24 ms of the cards' 40, and the cards' memory rate sets them, not the kernel: 69 rows read 86 % of every bank when the sessions route apart. Read Nov 9 on an RTX 5080 (960 GB/s) at a card's quarter of a layer's bank (`test-backend-ops perf -o MUL_MAT_ID`, 288 experts, 8 used, 69 rows): gate or up 258 us and down 338 us in IQ3_XXS (768 and 586 GB/s), 344 and 358 us in NVFP4 (846 and 812 GB/s), 658 and 716 us in Q8_0 (835 and 768 GB/s). A layer is 854 us in IQ3_XXS and 1,047 us in NVFP4: NVFP4's 4.5 bits are 1.47 times IQ3_XXS's bytes and its kernel moves them 1.1 to 1.4 times as fast. By memory rate to an RTX PRO 6000 (1,597 GB/s Server, 1,792 Workstation), the target's 42 banks are 19-22 ms in IQ3_XXS and 24-26 ms in NVFP4. A pack with NVFP4 experts (the expert format of `orcarouter/GLM-5.3-Flash-Uncensored-NVFP4`; `conversion/base.py` repacks `nvfp4-pack-quantized` experts into `GGML_TYPE_NVFP4`, not run on that checkpoint) would be 4.3-4.9 ms a step slower at 23 sessions and 13.7 GB a card larger. The MTP block's bank is Q8_0: 2.2-2.5 ms a step (two proposals at 23 rows, the catch-up at 69), and 1.2-1.3 in NVFP4. The smaller formats do not bank their bytes in this engine: at the same 69 rows IQ2_S takes 387 and 489 us (1,262 us a layer) and IQ2_XXS 311 and 306 us (927), 35-45 % of the card's memory rate where IQ3_XXS's tile reaches 60-80 %, so IQ3_XXS is the fastest of the five formats here (another job shared the card for part of that run; its IQ3_XXS rows read as the clean ones). Unmeasured: the sessions' real routing, and whether a 4.5-bit target accepts more drafts (NVFP4 experts break even at 70 % of drafts accepted against today's 58-62).

## Known causes, each a number

1. **Host work that grows with the sessions, on one thread** (the cause of the 150 ms; about 5.5 ms a session a step). Two parts:
   - **The step's graphs are built again as the shared pool grows** (fixed in the engine, not read on cards yet). A pool that sequences share is padded to 256 cells a sequence, the next power of two, so it crosses as often a step as one sequence does (`LLAMA_KV_PAD_SHARED_LEGACY=1` keeps 256). On the local fixture at 23 sessions one step of 91 builds a graph where 22 of 90 did, and a step averages 30-34 ms where it averaged 37-46 (four and five runs; other work shares that card and CPU).
   - **A rollback read every cell of the pool, for each session, each step** (fixed in the engine, not read on cards yet). The first session to roll back in a step lowers the pool's head to its freed cell; the cells the others placed in that step are then above the head, and the walk for them went down to cell 0 and came back from the pool's last cell. On the local fixture at 23 sessions that was 3.7 ms of a step's host work at 131,072 cells over a 11,776-cell pool's (2.5 ms verifying, 1.2 drafting), and it grows with the pool: in a straight line from those two pools, about 16 ms a step at the 524,288 cells the box ran and about 190 ms at the 6,029,312 the goal needs. Now the cells just above the head are read first and none above the last used cell (`LLAMA_KV_SEQ_RM_HEAD_LEGACY=1` keeps the old walk): the same host work at 131,072 cells as at 11,776.
   - Expected on four cards with both: `alloc_graph` gone from the step, and 23 sessions at 45-60 ms a step, 36-48 tok/s a session at MTP depth 2.
   - **A verified row's candidates, read on the server's thread** (fixed in the engine, not read on cards yet). Of the fixture's 32 ms step at 23 sessions and short histories the verify stage was 5 ms, and 3 ms of it was `common_sampler_top_k_candidates` for one row a session: about 130 us a row for the 296 candidates the served chain reads (top-k 40 behind a penalty window of 256) out of 154,880 logits. Stack samples by address put it in three places: a heap of the 296 that took a pop and a push for each of about 1,850 logits, a block test the compiler left as sixteen scalar compares (`common` is built without AVX), and a second look at every logit of a block that passed. Now the logits above a bar wait in a buffer of 2k that keeps its k largest when it fills, the bar starts at the k-th largest of the row's block maxima so the buffer is cut once, the block test is four SSE2 compares, and only the logits above the bar are looked at again (`LLAMA_TOP_K_HEAP_LEGACY=1` keeps the heap). The candidates are the ones a sort of every token keeps, in its order. Read Nov 9 on the fixture at 23 sessions, in turn with the heap: the verify stage 1.26 to 1.30 ms where the heap reads 3.59 to 3.73, the step 25.4 ms where it reads 27.9 to 28.0. The box verifies about two rows a session. Read and not the fix: sampling on the cards (`--backend-sampling`) takes the verify stage to 0.4 ms and adds 6.5 ms to the target's decode, a step of 33.3 ms against 32.1; the backend's top-k alone, for a chain with no penalty, adds 3.0-3.6 ms to the decode at k 40 and 1.4 ms at k 128 for 1.5 ms of verify taken; reading the rows' candidates on worker threads ahead of the loop took longer than the loop on a CPU other work shares two to three ways (verify 6.4-8.6 ms against 5.0-5.4) and was not kept.
   - **The MTP head's draft samplers took a seed from the system's random device at every reset**, one a sequence a draft (fixed in the engine): 0.56 ms a step at 23 sessions, the step's first host stage 0.65 ms and 0.09 with a fixed seed. A draft is its row's first candidate, so no draw decides it.
   - **The output rows, copied whole to the host and cut there** (fixed in the engine on the fixture, not read on cards yet). A decode copied every output row to the host: 69 rows of 154,880 logits a step at 23 sessions, 43 MB and 1.47 ms of the fixture's 16.2 ms target decode. The server's thread then cut one row a session to the 296 candidates its chain reads (49 us a row on the fixture; the 3.7 ms verify stage on the box), and each of the MTP head's proposals built a top-k sampler graph a sequence. Now a decode ends in one node that takes each row's k largest on the cards, each card from its share of the vocab with no gather, and the host reads those in place of the rows (`llama_set_output_candidates`; `LLAMA_OUTPUT_CANDIDATES_LEGACY=1` keeps rows whole and the chains). Read Nov 10 on the fixture at 23 sessions, three passes in turn with the switch: a step 19.8 to 19.9 ms where it was 22.8 to 23.3, verify 0.52 to 0.55 ms where 1.39 to 1.52, the target's decode 14.6 ms where 15.6 to 16.1, a proposal's stage 2.14 to 2.19 ms where 2.38 to 2.49. The candidates are the ones a cut of the whole row gives, ids and logit bits (4,096 rows on the card at k 10 and k 296). A slot whose request needs the whole row (probabilities before sampling, a grammar or the reasoning budget once it constrains) still reads it, and a ubatch with one such slot copies its rows. Read and not the fix: scanning the rows on helper threads ahead of the loop (the rows arrive by DMA and the scan is bound by memory: 116 to 125 us a row on each of eight threads against 49 on one). Not taken apart yet: a proposal's stage is 2.15 ms on the fixture for a 23-row pass of one block, on the card, while the 69-row catch-up hides inside 0.5 ms of verify.
   - **A dense pool's 256 padding** (fixed in the engine on the fixture, not read on cards yet). The first part above pads the pool to a power of two once it scores. Below the 2,051 cells its indexer selects it reads densely and kept 256, so 23 sessions from short prompts crossed it every five steps until the pool scored: two of the four slow groups in a session's first 18 steps on the box (steps 6-7 and 10-11, +47 and +70 ms on a 59 ms step). Now a shared pool that reads densely is padded to the next power of two that still reads densely (`LLAMA_KV_PAD_DENSE_LEGACY=1` keeps 256). On a fixture with a dense phase (the indexer's top_k at 2,048, Nov 10) the 69-row target graph is built 3 times where it was built 6. Left in a session's first steps: the first build after the prompts, and the switch to scoring, which builds the target graph twice. Sessions with long histories score from their first step and gain nothing.
   - **A step whose set of decoding sessions changed builds its graphs again** (open; cause found Nov 10). A graph is reused only by a step with the same sequences in it (`llm_graph_params::allow_reuse`, `ubatch_shape_or_sequences`), a context keeps its last graph and three slots, and a new graph runs once step by step and is captured on its second evaluation. So when sessions end one at a time every step is a new shape. On the box (Nov 10, a warm `rate.py` pass at 23 sessions) the 24 steps after the first session ends take 2,415 ms, 100 to 139 ms each at 66 down to 3 rows where a 69-row step is 59, and the next pass's first three steps (101, 121 and 123 ms) build the 69-row shapes again. The fixture shows the same with sessions of staggered lengths: a count's first two steps are slow and its third replays, and a pass builds about 100 target graphs and 120 draft graphs, every pass. Read and not the fix: a slot a shape (48 slots a context and as many captures in the meta backend). It built as many graphs a pass as three slots do, since a pass goes through four to five times more shapes than it has counts of sessions. A slot's buffers are sized by the pool and not by its rows (519 MiB a card at 32 rows and 546 at 69 on the box), so holding a pass's shapes would take tens of GiB a card; host memory grew by 8 GiB on the fixture; and a slot's late allocation ran a 16 GB card out of memory, which aborts the server (`ggml-backend-meta.cpp`, `GGML_ASSERT(bufs.back() != nullptr)`; main allocates its three slots late too). What would fix it, not built: keep the step's shape while sessions come and go, with rows that stand in for a session that left (no cells written, outputs dropped) until the set has stayed smaller for a while.
   - **The head's graph changes form when its drafts can take the target's selection** (open; found Nov 10 on the fixture). `llama_kpool_draft::can_reuse` lets the head read the selection the target kept only while every drafting row is still in the pool its captured row was in (`kpool` 4): for the second proposal that fails two steps of every four when the sessions advance together. The head's context is allowed one graph slot (`slots_allowed=1` in `GGML_GRAPH_TRACE=1`), so each change of form builds the 23-row graph again and captures it two steps later: 30 draft graph builds in 54 steps, the second proposal's decode call at 1.6 to 1.8 ms where it is 0.3, and a step about 1.2 ms longer. It is in the fixture runs where all 23 sessions hold a captured row and not in the runs where one does not (3 builds), which is why fixture passes of the same binary read in two groups. With 23 real sessions advancing by different amounts a step, all rows rarely stay in their pools at once, so the box at 23 sessions is mostly on the scoring form; one to four sessions meet the change often. Not fixed.
2. **Passes per step** (fixed on main for the shared pool). Every ubatch is a full weight read: `9ca2776d0`, `861d22f10`, `e18d70a0b` for MTP depth and the output limit, `a3eff8478` pads any shorter draft to the step's longest. `dp-4`'s private streams still split on idle slots between busy ones.
3. **The pool for 23 sessions of 256K does not load.** At `-ub 2048` the graph reserve asks for a compute buffer that grows with the pool: 31.8 GiB on a card at 6,029,312 tokens, on top of 48 GiB of cache and 32 GiB of weights. A 524,288-token pool loads with 48.6 GiB used a card. What the reserve costs a pool token, and at which batch step, has two measurements and needs a third to be a rule.
4. **`dp-4` crashes at 4 and 12 sessions** (Nov 5). The split-state stack overflow has a fix on main (`ea2201d80`), not run on cards since.
5. **Host work that grows with the cells the pool holds** (fixed in the engine on the fixture, not read on cards yet). A shared pool's sequences were more than one to a stream, so `llama_kpool_set_input` took none of them from its resident view and built the indexer's pool maps and mask facts from the cells at every decode: for each session it walked every cell of the pool several times and wrote two integers a cell (`kpool_mask_cells` was n_kv x 2 x sessions), and the cards scored every row against every session's pools. On the fixture at 23 sessions (Nov 9, `LLAMA_TIME_DECODE=1`, the last 64 decodes of a run) `set_inputs` was 0.85 ms a decode at 8,192 cells (48-token prompts), 1.62 ms at 32,768 (1,000-token prompts) and 6.38 ms at 131,072 (4,000-token prompts, the fixture's longest), four decodes a step: steps of 32.4, 34.8 and 63.8 ms. Now each sequence of the ubatch takes a stream of pool maps of its own over the one cache stream, served from its view, with its table on the cards in one bank patched with what a step changed (`LLAMA_KPOOL_SHARED_VIEWS_LEGACY=1` keeps the packed maps). Read Nov 9 on a quiet machine, the same fixture and prompts: `set_inputs` 0.62 to 0.68, 0.58 to 0.64 and 0.58 to 0.83 ms a decode, steps of 28.0 to 28.8, 27.9 to 29.3 and 28.0 to 28.6 ms, where the packed maps read 27.4, 34.0 and 60.9 ms that evening. The step at 4,000 tokens a session is the step at 48. The logits are those of a cache a sequence, byte for byte on the CPU. They are not the packed maps', as this said until Nov 10: the test behind it compared nothing (the switch was read after the test had put it back, fixed), and read for real 28 of its 960 rows differ between the packed maps and a cache a sequence by up to 0.5% of the largest logit. What it costs:
   - **Memory**: the bank is 14 bytes a pool cell a slot on every card, and the views 13.5 in host memory: 1.94 GB a card and 1.87 GB of host memory for 23 slots on 6,029,312 cells, on top of cause 3.
   - **The bank's patch writes** (fixed in the engine on the fixture, not read on cards yet). What a step changed went to the bank as 241 small writes a decode at 23 sessions, each copied to every rank, and they were all of `set_inputs`. Now a decode's changes go as one graph input that each rank writes into its bank before the maps are made (`LLAMA_KPOOL_PATCH_INPUT_LEGACY=1` keeps the writes). Read Nov 10 on the fixture at 48, 1,000 and 4,000 tokens a session: `set_inputs` 0.009 to 0.015 ms a decode where the writes take 0.57 to 0.62, steps of 23.4 to 23.7, 23.1 and 23.3 to 23.4 ms where they take 26.5, 25.4 to 25.8 and 25.7 to 25.9. The logits are those with the writes, byte for byte on the CPU.

## The layout

`tensor-4`: four-way tensor split (`-sm tensor -ts 1,1,1,1`, every card on every request) with one shared unified pool. It is the layout the reads run on because it is one pass a step whichever slots are busy, with no engine change. It ran on four real cards for the first time on Nov 9 (a fixture of the pack's attention shape, then the pack). `dp-4` (experts split across cards, private 442,368-cell slots) stays in the tree. The rig lead's work on it (Nov 7 to 9: one draft depth a concurrent round, the routed experts' gate and up prepared together, MTP hidden rows kept on the devices, prompt chunks, captures kept by scheduler generation) sat uncommitted until Nov 10 and is on main since `8832d8669`. It read 25 to 28 tok/s a session at 36 sessions on four cards, 920 to 1,030 tok/s over the cards, where `tensor-4`'s 23 sessions at 30 are 690. The two were never read at the same sessions, prompts and engine: `dp-4` at 23 sessions is a number the next box read can give.

- Shapes divide four ways on whole IQ3_XXS blocks: expert and shared feed-forward 2,048, dense 12,288, 64 heads, 32 indexer heads, vocabulary 154,880. A fixture with fewer heads than ranks asserts in the meta backend; the pack does not.
- The DSA latent cache and the indexer cache are mirrored on every card under `-sm tensor`.
- Load takes 67-74 s from a warm page cache.
- The split does not move the routed experts, which are most of a step's reads: every split reads each touched expert once. A card's 72 whole experts under `-sm dp` take what its quarter of all 288 takes under `-sm tensor` (Nov 9, RTX 5080, IQ3_XXS, `test-backend-ops perf -o MUL_MAT_ID_PARTITION`: gate or up 289 us and down 291 us at 36 rows, 346 and 354 us at 108, 654-777 GB/s; about 837 us a layer at 69 rows against the quarter's 854). What a split moves, by `scripts/glm53/estimate_decode_pcie.py`'s byte model at 23 sessions, MTP depth 2, 400K histories and the cards' rated 1,597 GB/s: `dp-4` reads for 26.0 ms a step (its dense weights are read on every card, 9.2 GB a pass against a quarter's 2.3) and transfers for 1.7 ms as designed or 6.4 as coded; `tensor-4` reads for 21-23 ms by the same model (23 if every card scans every session's index keys, which its mirrored caches allow); shared dense weights with a session's caches on one card would read for 21. The experts are 17.8 ms of each. `tensor-4`'s cards measured 40 ms. `dp-4` keeps a session's caches on one card, which is the memory the 23 x 256K pool lacks (cause 3), not a faster step.
- Closed Nov 9: on the CPU, four tensor-split ranks returned NaN logits when the cache length was padded well past its used cells. A rank with no slice of a split weight (the fixture has fewer heads than ranks) never computes that node, and the all-reduce zeroed its unwritten buffer by scaling it by 0.0, which keeps a NaN. It is filled with zeros now (`GGML_META_ZERO_SCALE_LEGACY=1` scales), and `test-speculative-mtp`'s prepared-weights check runs without the 256-cell padding it kept for this. The pack gives every card a slice, so the goal's path never took it.

## Launch

The launch is in this fork and needs nothing outside it. `scripts/glm53/serve.py` holds every server argument and
environment setting of the layout; `scripts/glm53/rate.py` reads the number from the running server.

```sh
python3 scripts/glm53/serve.py --profile tensor-4 \
    --server <build>/bin/llama-server \
    --model <pack>/GLM-5.3-Flash-Uncensored-IQ3_XXS-00001-of-00003.gguf
python3 scripts/glm53/rate.py --sessions 23
```

- `tensor-4` is `-sm tensor -ts 1,1,1,1 --kv-unified`, 23 slots on one pool of 524,288 tokens (the pool measured to
  load), the 4-bit latent cache, MTP depth 2. `--slots`, `--context`, `--mtp-depth 1..4`, `--mtp-adaptive`, `--backend-sampling` and
  `--dflash FILE --dflash-depth N` change one thing each; `--dry-run` prints the argv and environment as JSON.
- The pack: `BoldingBuilds/orcarouter_GLM-5.3-Flash-Uncensored-GGUF` at revision
  `1bc0afadd92f4ff9fc2b1281a95a7921b058d35d`, the three `IQ3_XXS/` shards in one directory (134.3 GB).
- The engine: this fork with CUDA for the cards' compute capability, `120a` for RTX PRO 6000:
  `cmake -B build -G Ninja -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DGGML_CUDA=ON -DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=120a -DGGML_CUDA_FA=ON
  -DGGML_CUDA_GRAPHS=ON -DLLAMA_BUILD_SERVER=ON -DLLAMA_BUILD_UI=OFF -DLLAMA_USE_PREBUILT_UI=OFF` and
  `cmake --build build --target llama-server`.
- The DFlash2 draft: `Anbeeld/GLM-5.3-Flash-DFlash2-GGUF` at `f3569acb7f9cee95e66d24442d75536eb00ab842`,
  `GLM-5.3-Flash-DFlash2-Q8_0.gguf`. As published it holds neither token embeddings nor an output head and reads the
  target's, which a tensor-split target keeps where no draft context can run: the server aborts at load.
  `scripts/glm53/dflash_own_head.py --draft FILE --target <the three shards> --out FILE` writes the draft with its own
  copies, and `--dflash` takes that file. Its cache is its own 2,048-token window a session plus one step, about 1 GiB
  at 23 sessions whatever the pool holds.

On vast.ai, rig rents the box, pulls the pack by its hashes and ships the engine build; the launch is still the two
lines above.
